# Why this visualization is Bad; 
# I chose the Polar Area Chart example as bad because it a.makes comparison hard, b.risk of distortion when viewing, and c.does not follow strong perceptual design principles.
# 1 = labels are harder to read in circular formats. Users may need to continue looking and interpreting around the chart instead of looking in a straight line. Knaflic (2015) emphasizes reducing cognitive effort and guiding the viewer’s eye logically. A circular format does not always support this.
# 2 = the radial charts used include decorative design elements that prioritize aesthetics over clarity. While visually interesting, they can distract from the data. Tufte (1983) refers to this as “chartjunk,” which adds non-essential ink without increasing understanding
# 3 = accessibility is limited. Color is used as the main differentiator between segments. When contrast is low, users with color vision challenges may have difficulty to interpret the data (Ware, 2013).
# 4 = the polar area charts rely on comparing area and angles, which is difficult to judge accurately. Cleveland and McGill (1984) found that length and position are easier to interpret than area. Because values are shown in a circular format, it becomes hard to compare categories accuractly
# 5 = the circular layout  visually distorts differences. Categories placed farther from the center look larger due to radial expansion. This can exaggerate differences in a way that is not always obvious. Tufte (1983) says that visualizations should minimize distortion and avoid increasing the “lie factor.” The polar area chart does not follow this logic.
